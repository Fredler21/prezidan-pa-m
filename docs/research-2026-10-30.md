### 1c. Eligibility rules the tracker states
- Constitution art. 135 plus the 2026 electoral rules ("Electoral Decree of June 2, 2026, as modified").
- Sponsoring party/grouping must have registered 30,000 supporters; an independent presidential candidate needs 150,000 voter signatures; registration fee 2,000,000 HTG (HPH: about US$15,300, "approximate").
- (HaitiLibre, 3rd revision of the decree, 6 Oct 2026: fee unchanged for president; Senate fee cut to 400,000 HTG, deputies to 100,000 HTG.) https://www.haitilibre.com/article-48643-haiti-elections-3eme-revision-du-decret-electoral-details-des-modifications-pdf.html ; decree PDF (79 pp.) https://www.haitilibre.com/docs/decret-electoral-06-10-2026.pdf

### 1d. Sanctions and the October 2026 rule change (relevant to Martelly, Privert, Bodeau)
- The 2 June 2026 decree (Moniteur spécial n°27), art. 153 al. 20, required a BRH certificate that the candidate is "ni débiteur insolvable, ni failli, ni interdit de chéquier…" and declared inadmissible persons sanctioned by the UN Security Council, OFAC or other foreign governments (Amitié FM, 11 Jun 2026, https://www.amitiefm.ht/post/l-article-153-du-d%C3%A9cret-%C3%A9lectoral-interdit-aux-sanctionn%C3%A9s-de-se-pr%C3%A9senter-aux-%C3%A9lections ; HaitiLibre above).
- The Primature's revised decree of 6 Oct 2026 deleted art. 153 al. 20 and 21 (BRH, ONA and FDI certificates and the foreign-sanctions inadmissibility); UN-Security-Council-sanctioned persons remain excluded (HaitiLibre above; AP/La 1ère 18 Oct 2026, which dates the published change to 8 Oct 2026: https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/guadeloupe/haiti-assouplit-les-regles-electorales-pour-permettre-aux-personnalites-sanctionnees-par-les-etats-unis-ou-l-europe-de-se-presenter-1748901.html ). Critics quoted: Michael Deibert (safeguards insufficient), former PM Evans Paul (repeated changes erode trust). Le Nouvelliste, 13 Oct 2026 ("Les prochaines élections, les sanctionnés et les pays amis", https://lenouvelliste.com/article/272923/the-upcoming-elections-the-sanctioned-and-the-friendly-countries ) reports Canada does not want sanctioned persons to take part (article paywalled; headline-level only).
- HPH tracker caveats: "None identified" is limited to the sources it reviewed and "is not an exhaustive worldwide sanctions check"; sanctions and criminal convictions are distinct; the indicator "does not determine candidate eligibility". HPH sources: Canada SOR/2022-226 schedule; US Treasury jy1389 (Bodeau) and jy2542 (Martelly); EU Council 15 Dec 2025 listing; EU Council 26 Jun 2026 extension of the Haiti framework to July 2027.

## 4. The two former presidents: term records

### 4a. Michel Joseph Martelly — President, 14 May 2011 – 7 Feb 2016

Baseline: born 12 Feb 1961; konpa musician known as "Sweet Micky"; 56th head of state per Le Nouvelliste; won the 20 Mar 2011 runoff with 716,986 votes / 67.57% (HPH "Previous Presidential Elections" table; CRS: ~68% of votes cast, about 15% of registered voters given low turnout). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Martelly ; CRS R42559 "Haiti Under President Martelly: Current Conditions and Congressional Concerns" (updated 23 Dec 2015) https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R42559.html (also https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R42559/R42559.30.pdf ).

1. **Inauguration and the first peaceful opposition hand-over (14 May 2011).** Sworn in for a five-year term; CRS and Wikipedia both describe it as the first peaceful transfer of power between presidents of opposing parties in Haitian history. [CRS R42559; Wikipedia]
2. **Prime-minister instability.** Took about five months to get a PM confirmed: Garry Conille confirmed 4 Oct 2011, resigned Feb 2012; Laurent Lamothe named May 2012, resigned 14 Dec 2014 under pressure over the electoral impasse; Evans Paul became the fifth PM in early 2015 but parliament dissolved before confirming him. [CRS R42559; Wikipedia]
3. **Electoral council and elections not held.** Fired the CEP in Dec 2011 and did not start forming a new one until June 2012, six months after one-third of the Senate was due for renewal; the Senate fell to 20 members in May 2012 and struggled for quorum; the government missed its pledge to hold elections by end-2012, and no legislative elections were held until Aug 2015. [CRS IF10440 (6 Jan 2017) https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2017-01-06_IF10440_d49e98ffe990d855fa8f713856ea6dcf4c6bfe96.pdf ; CRS R42559]
4. **2012 constitutional amendments published (June 2012).** The government published amendments passed by two legislatures, including voting rights for dual citizens, a 30% quota for women in government posts and a streamlined process for a permanent electoral council. [CRS R42559; CRS IF10440]
5. **Caracol Industrial Park opened (22 Oct 2012).** Northern Haiti park, US/IDB-backed (about US$300 million), inaugurated with Secretary Hillary Clinton and Bill Clinton; Martelly: "Caracol is evidence that Haiti is irreversibly open for business"; job projections ranged 37,000–60,000; about 13,000 employed by Apr 2019. [US State Dept remarks https://2009-2017.state.gov/secretary/20092013clinton/rm/2012/10/199451.htm ; Dallas Morning News 23 Oct 2012 https://www.dallasnews.com/news/2012/10/23/clintons-land-in-haiti-to-showcase-industrial-park/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracol,_Haiti ]
6. **Free schooling (PSUGO) and the National Education Fund.** Flagship campaign promise; the Fonds National de l'Éducation was announced days after inauguration (May 2011), financed by levies on international phone calls and diaspora money transfers; the ministry claimed over one million pupils covered at about US$43 million a year; a 2013 Haiti Support Group investigation found late or missing payments to schools, missing books, weak supervision, a fraud case in Port-de-Paix and "phantom" schools; critics called the levies illegal because parliament had not approved the fund (a law creating the FNE was only published in 2017). [Haiti Support Group, 13 Feb 2013 https://haitisupportgroup.org/the-government-qfree-schoolq-program-a-victory/ ; CRS R42559 (describes the free-education initiative funded by phone and remittance taxes)]
7. **PetroCaribe-funded programmes and later audits.** Social programmes such as Ede Pèp / Ti Manman Cheri (run through FAES) and many infrastructure contracts were funded from Venezuela's PetroCaribe facility; the Cour Supérieure des Comptes' second audit report (31 May 2019, covering Sep 2008–Sep 2016) found collusion, favouritism and embezzlement in the fund's projects, and VOA reported that audits found about US$3.8 billion of PetroCaribe revenue unaccounted for; the US State Department sanctioned ex-PM Lamothe in June 2023, alleging he misappropriated at least US$60 million from the fund (he called the accusation "preposterous"). Note: a commentary estimate that roughly two-thirds of the fund was spent under Martelly is opinion, not an audited figure. [CSCCA report 2 https://cscca.gouv.ht/uploads/documents/1748211644_petrocaribe_rapport_2_31_mai_2019.pdf ; VOA 7 Jun 2019 https://www.voanews.com/a/haiti-braces-for-anti-corruption-protests-sunday-/4950920.html ; CNN via KTVZ 2 Jun 2023 https://ktvz.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2023/06/02/state-department-sanctions-former-haitian-prime-minister-lamothe-for-corruption/ ]
8. **Army revival.** Announced in Aug 2011 a plan to reinstate the military disbanded in 1995, criticised by human-rights activists; CRS reports the government revived the army with a stated focus on border patrol, anti-smuggling and disaster response. [Wikipedia; CRS R42559]
9. **Corruption allegations and protests.** 2012 allegations of US$2.6 million in bribes linked to a Dominican construction company (denied); CRS records investigations involving advisers and family members, including his wife Sophia Martelly being rejected as a Senate candidate (2015), and allegations around post-earthquake contracts; anti-government protests over living costs and corruption from Nov 2013; the Apr 2015 release of Woodly Ethéart, an indicted kidnapping-ring leader with ties to the presidential family, drew criticism. Oct 2013 "Île-à-Vache" US$20 billion development plan came to nothing. [Wikipedia; CRS R42559]
10. **Parliament lapses; rule by decree (12 Jan 2015 – 7 Feb 2016).** With no elections held, the terms of the remaining senators and the whole Chamber expired on 12 Jan 2015; Martelly governed by decree for the rest of his term and installed a fifth CEP on 23 Jan 2015. [CRS R42559; CRS IF10440; Gulf News/AFP https://gulfnews.com/world/americas/haiti-enters-uncertain-political-phase-as-parliament-dissolved-1.1440983 ]
11. **The 2015 election cycle.** First-round legislative elections 9 Aug 2015 (turnout ~18%; voting invalidated in ~13% of polling centres; violence closed ~5% of stations); presidential first round and legislative runoffs 25 Oct 2015 (turnout ~26%); Martelly backed Jovenel Moïse (PHTK); opposition alleged fraud; Martelly swore in an evaluation commission on 22 Dec 2015; the 27 Dec 2015 runoff was postponed and then the 24 Jan 2016 date was abandoned after protests and an opposition boycott. [CRS R42559; UN News 15 Feb 2016 https://news.un.org/en/node/522252 ]
12. **Departure without an elected successor (7 Feb 2016).** Signed a last-minute accord (5/6 Feb 2016) with Senate president Privert and Chamber president Cholzer Chancy for a parliament-chosen interim president; left office on 7 Feb 2016; in May–June 2016 an independent verification commission found the 2015 vote "dishonest" and the CEP annulled the October 2015 presidential results. [UN News above; VOA/Reuters 6 Jun 2016 https://www.voanews.com/a/haiti-scraps-election-interim-president-says-could-stay-for-months/3364859.html ; Wikipedia]
13. **Post-presidency (for context, not term record).** Returned to music (2016 carnival song mocking journalist Liliane Pierre-Paul); Canada sanctions 17 Nov 2022; US sanctions 20 Aug 2024; EU sanctions 15 Dec 2025; ULCC accusation of misrepresenting assets; returned to Haiti 15 Jul 2026; registered for president under Debloke Oct 2026 (sections 1d and 3.10). [Wikipedia; Treasury jy2542; AP via WTOP]

### 4b. Jocelerme Privert — Provisional President, 14 Feb 2016 – 7 Feb 2017

Baseline: born 1 Feb 1953 in Petit-Trou-de-Nippes; accountant; career in the Direction Générale des Impôts 1979–1999; Secretary of State for Finance 2001–02; Minister of the Interior and Territorial Communities 15 Mar 2002 – 12 Mar 2004 under Jean-Bertrand Aristide; arrested 4 Apr 2004 over alleged involvement in the Feb 2004 La Scierie (Saint-Marc) killings and held about 26 months without a trial concluding; later adviser to René Préval; lost a 2008 Senate by-election, elected senator for Nippes in 2010 (served 26 Apr 2011 – 14 Jan 2016, chaired the Senate economy and finance committee); Senate president 14 Jan – 14 Feb 2016. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jocelerme_Privert

1. **The February 2016 accord and parliamentary election (5/6 and 14 Feb 2016).** As Senate president he co-signed the accord with Martelly and Cholzer Chancy; the National Assembly elected him provisional president on 14 Feb 2016 (second ballot 77 votes to 33 for Edgard Leblanc Fils) for a mandate of up to 120 days, with a presidential runoff planned for 24 Apr and hand-over on 14 May 2016; the UN Secretary-General called it a "crucial first step". [UN News 15 Feb 2016 https://news.un.org/en/node/522252 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2016_Haitian_presidential_election ; Caribbean National Weekly 15 Mar 2016 https://www.caribbeannationalweekly.com/posts/haitis-interim-president-names-new-electoral-council ]
2. **Government formation.** The Chamber rejected the policy statement of his first PM-designate Fritz Jean on 20 Mar 2016; he appointed Enex Jean-Charles on 22 Mar 2016, ratified by both chambers; government installed 28 Mar 2016 (Jean-Charles served to 21 Mar 2017). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enex_Jean-Charles ; CARICOM Today https://today.caricom.org/?p=14295 ]
3. **New electoral council (13 Mar 2016).** Named a new nine-member CEP, later led by Léopold Berlanger. [Caribbean National Weekly above; NPR 14 Oct 2016]
4. **Independent verification commission and annulment of the 2015 results (May–June 2016).** His commission audited about 13,000 ballots, concluded the 2015 vote was "dishonest" (fraud, "zombie" voting) and recommended a full rerun; on 6 Jun 2016 the CEP annulled the Oct 2015 presidential results and scheduled a new first round for 9 Oct 2016 and runoff for 8 Jan 2017; PHTK called it an "electoral coup"; the US warned the restart could prolong the absence of an elected president. [VOA/Reuters 6 Jun 2016 above; CRS IF10440]
5. **Overstaying the 120-day mandate (from 14 Jun 2016).** His mandate expired 14 Jun 2016; parliament repeatedly failed to vote on extending it or naming a replacement; he said he would stay until an elected successor took office "unless parliament decides otherwise"; the Obama administration continued to recognise him. [CRS IF10440; VOA/Reuters above; CARICOM Today https://today.caricom.org/?p=15746 ]
6. **Hurricane Matthew and the postponed vote (Oct 2016).** Matthew struck on 4 Oct 2016 (at least 500 deaths); the 9 Oct election was postponed and on 14 Oct the CEP set 20 Nov 2016 for the first round and 29 Jan 2017 for runoffs. [NPR 14 Oct 2016 https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2016/10/14/497990449/haiti-presidential-election-rescheduled-for-november ]
7. **The 20 Nov 2016 election and results (3 Jan 2017).** Vote held on 20 Nov 2016 (27 presidential candidates; turnout 21%); Jovenel Moïse (PHTK) won outright with about 55.6% (590,927 votes per HPH table), Jude Célestin ~20%, Moïse Jean-Charles 11%, Maryse Narcisse 9%; after complaints and appeals the CEP published final results on 3 Jan 2017; no runoff needed. [CRS IF10440; HPH tracker "Previous Presidential Elections"]
8. **Hand-over (7 Feb 2017).** Transferred power to Jovenel Moïse on the constitutional date, ending almost a year without an elected president; the US State Department welcomed the results as a step back to "full constitutional rule". [CRS IF10440; Wikipedia]
9. **Criticism during the term.** Opposition (PHTK) accused him of manipulating the CEP to engineer a rerun; international observers had disagreed on the need to re-run the vote; his continued tenure after 14 Jun 2016 lacked a parliamentary vote. [CRS IF10440; VOA/Reuters]
10. **Post-presidency (context).** Canada sanctioned him on 15/16 Feb 2023 for "acts of significant corruption" and alleged support to gangs (SOR/2023-25); he has disputed the sanctions and, in Jan 2024, denounced an indictment by judge Al Duniel Dimanche as illegitimate; registered for president under Ayiti Transfòme in Oct 2026. [Canada Gazette above; Le Nouvelliste 5 Jan 2024 above; Karibinfo 21 Oct 2026]

## 5. Tracker methodology, neutrality and definitions

- **What it is.** Page title "Haiti Election Tracker: 2026–2027 Electoral Process"; listed under HPH's "Data and Policy Lab". Sections (in order): voting requirements (citizenship/age, national ID, CEP registration, political rights); "What about the diaspora?" (procedures pending; registration and voting sites abroad not finalised); access to registration/voting centres nationwide and in gang-controlled territory (map of gang-influenced communes; commune table of voting-age population vs. voting centres, e.g. Port-au-Prince 681,481 / 11 centres; Carrefour 340,878 / 0; Cité Soleil 174,571 / 0; Croix-des-Bouquets 163,434 / 0; IHSI 2024 population estimates; IOM May 2026: ~1.47 million internally displaced); political groupings and parties; candidate eligibility; previous presidential elections (2000 Aristide 2,632,534 / 91.81%; 2006 Préval 992,766 / 51.21%; 2011 Martelly 716,986 / 67.57% round 2; 2016 Moïse 590,927 / 55.60%; annulled 2015 cycle excluded); presidential candidates; sources and further reading; "Live News"; newsletter; donation form. Photos credited to the Government of Haiti.
- **Probability statement.** "HPH assigns a 90% probability" to first-round elections proceeding on 18 Jul 2027; the page says this "reflects HPH's institutional judgment", "is not a statistically derived forecast or a guarantee that elections will occur", and "is subject to revision as new evidence emerges".
- **Calendar disclaimer.** Dates "indicate the official schedule, not verified completion"; passing a deadline "does not confirm implementation"; the CEP may revise them.
- **Candidate disclaimer.** Registration "remains subject to CEP verification and validation and does not establish final approval".
- **Sanctions disclaimer.** "None identified in the sources reviewed" is "limited to those sources and is not an exhaustive worldwide sanctions check"; sanctions and criminal convictions are distinct; the indicator "does not determine candidate eligibility".
- **Registration-data disclaimer (Oct brief).** "Not verified" does not mean participation never occurred; the six-million-voter figure is "an analytical benchmark, not a legal prerequisite for elections" (HPH attributes the 6M target to the Haitian Government). HPH's own series: 846,446 applications 20 Jul–6 Oct 2026; peak 24,931/day on 17 Aug; 30-day average ~9,516/day; ~759,000 confirmed as of 6 Oct; at current pace the 6M benchmark would be reached in Apr 2028.
- **Neutrality.** The tracker contains **no explicit neutrality or nonpartisan statement**, and HPH's home, About, Team and Board pages contain none either (home-page mission: "center Haitian expertise, prioritize community-informed solutions" and bridge grassroots realities and international decision-making). Entity: Haïti Policy House, Inc., 1100 13th Street NW, PMB 0803, Suite 800, Washington DC 20005; founder & CEO Wolf Pamphile; board president Johnny Celestin; research advisory board Robert Fatton Jr., Lovesun Parent, Sophie Rutenbar. https://www.haitipolicyhouse.org/ , /our-team , /board-of-directors
- **"Platform published".** The tracker **does not define or use** a "platform published" field.
